# Project Status Report 

## Introduction
The Group Documentation Project is a collaborative effort to create a technical document or documentation in a mock project management setting. Objectives of the project are to strengthen technical documentation skills, work in a group setting under time constraints and real-world circumstances, and create a usable finished product with deadlines, “client” requirements, and direct feedback. Our project began in August 2026 and is expected to end in December 2026. Project members include Chloe Brown, Amy Muro, James Rowland, and Carson Wright. 
## Project Overview 
Our project focuses on creating a user-friendly beginner digital grant guide website to help writers who need to write a grant for their non-profit organization as well as aid them in using grant resources correctly. In our usability test, out of the 5 participants, we received a 2.4/5 in navigation, and a 2.2 in information readability. We also had issues of confusion within the navigation bar, and three participants requested sample content. With our results, and feedback we are focusing on refining the written content within the website, specifically the 'Document Guidance' section in our website. We aim to ensure our navigation and structure stay consistent throughout our document. We aim to eliminate visual and structural friction that users defined in our testing. 
### Usability Testing Overview 
Usability testing was conducted remotely through a digital Google Survey and recruited five participants with varying amounts of experience in grant writing. Users were asked to share their experiences using the guide and evaluate the guide's navigation, readability, and visual appeal. 
### Usability Testing Results
A significant number of users found navigation confusing and redundant. Users asked for additional examples in the guide and for better, more clear navigation. Users praised the visual design of the website. These results were used to further improve the guide with specific improvements in navigation and additional examples.
### What We Have Completed 
Formulated Audience Focused Frameworks: Built a custom digital grant writing resource using HTML and CSS to ensure structural clarity and accessibility. 

Usability Evaluation: Conducted a usability test assessing navigation pathways, information architecture layout decisions and content for the grant website. 

Usability Test Report: Gathered the data from the survey results, and created a report of our findings, and our next steps. 

### Work In Progress
We are currently working on ensuring our website layout, headings, and content are presented correctly after receiving the comments of the usability testing. The following are key points of work that we are working on: 
1. Updating Headings and Labels: Rewriting a few of our headings on the navigation bar due to users requesting more descriptive headings.
2. Simplifying the Navigation: Ensuring that the links to the navigation bar aren't being repeated or confusing users when different headings redirect them to the same content. 
3. Refining Page Layouts: Tweaking our website layout so that users can find key information faster, and help users scan information quicker. 
4. Clarifying Guide Content (Budget Section): Expanding and checking our budget section, and editing the related issues with the _Common Proposal Form_
##   Conclusion 
In conclusion, our team developed a short-format beginner's guide website for grant writing from August through the present. We aimed to provide users with the information necessary to understand the grant writing process and independently create an application. To collaborate and compile content from multiple sources, we utilized GitHub, HTML, and Markdown. These tools, alongside online research and weekly meetings, kept the team aligned throughout the creation of the website, which we constructed and styled to present our content effectively. Subsequently, we conducted a remote usability test with five participants representing varying experience levels. Based on this initial test, we identified critical areas for improvement, including site navigation, page layouts, content clarification, and descriptive headings. So far we have been successful in the creation of this product along with testing and are documenting the status of our work throughout the semester.
