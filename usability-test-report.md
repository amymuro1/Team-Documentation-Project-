## 1. Summary
 1.1 By reviewing and applying an introductory digital grant application resource, we aim to lower the barrier to entry for nonprofits seeking funding. Nonprofits may have limited access to specialists in the grant writing field and can face a lack of funding application options. Our goals in this study are to review user sentiments towards the application process, observe how they apply their own information hierarchy to the concepts provided, and to distill textual feedback into actionable insights. In creating this study, we sought to collect users with and without grant writing experience to aggregate data from both the informed and uninformed perspectives. Feedback on the efficacy of our terminology, alongside language from our experienced users, informs our research questions further. In gaining perspective from the inexperienced users, we can see the applicability of our information architecture. Users are presented with a reference grant application resource alongside supplementary instructional material to scaffold understanding. A Google Form providing feedback boxes allows users to respond to presented scenarios to assess comprehension and application rate in relation to supplementary material.
## 2. Participant Profile & Methodology
 2.1 **Participant Demographics:** To evaluate the website's accessibility and usability across varying experience levels, we recruited a diverse cohort of five participants aged 18 and older through our professional and personal networks of friends and colleagues. Recognizing that grant writing for non-profit organizations is not limited to experienced writers, our sample intentionally included a mix of individuals who had a writing background and those who did not. This balance allowed us to gather critical insights from both seasoned writers and complete beginners, mirroring our target audience of non-profit staff, writers, and volunteers who may be tasked with writing a grant regardless of their prior experience.  
 
 2.2 **Test Environment:** The surveys were completed within three days, on their own. The survey time was about 15-30 minutes. There were three key tasks that were conducted. The first tasked prompted users to use the guide as a source. The second task prompted users to use the guide as a source to main components of the grant. The third task prompted users to give feedback on the general navigation of the website.

## 4. Prioritized Revision List
 4.1 In our initial testing, we found recurring themes as described in our user feedback. In our major revisions, we need to address the navigation of the webpage as there is repetition in tabulation. This will be addressed through linking the reference document rather than using an encoded version. The reference document includes its own labeling that was then translated into the final webpage. Introductory users struggled with orienting themselves within the documentation, requiring an update to how we structure our guidance. Visual flow was positively reviewed, so incorporating the positive aspects into our revision text will strengthen the page. There was mention of including a budget section example, which will likely be provided in the “Grant Writing in Action” section as to provide various applicable formats given the high variability in budget requirements. Updating the explanation on this section will help clarify the discrepancy. In expanding into other sections, we will review the ratings given to determine course of action built upon provided remarks.
