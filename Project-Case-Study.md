# Project Case Study

## Project Context CARSON
The Non-Profit Grant Writing Guide was created as an introduction for non-profit organizations that may be in the process of applying for grants but lack the specialized assistance of a hired grant writer. Based on a target non-profit, Flying B Animal Rescue and Sanctuary of Ennis, TX, we created a model in which an organization could reasonably read through our guide and complete a grant application. Building upon the “Common Proposal Form” introduced to one of our team members during a previous grant writing class, we sought to address the need for clarification and guidance in completing the methodical grant process. We hope to expand access to the grant application process so that more organizations have the opportunity to acquire funding for their causes. 
## User Need JAMES
Our intended audience was people interested in grant writing, including people who are new to grant writing as well as those with experience. This audience required a guide that was written to suit varying levels of experience that is accessible to novices. 
## Research Methods CHLOE
How did we gather evidence and insights to understand our audience and guide our design decisions?

We held meetings and reviewed our content and sources to make informed decisions for our users.The team ran a usability test to gather insights and better understand our audience. Six participants both with and without grant writing experience completed the test on a Google survey platform to give feedback on our website. Afterward, we compiled the results to focus on areas the audience found confusing or lacking in content. We then updated the site and its content through our weekly meetings and direct messages.

We made our design choices after agreeing to feature short form content on the site. To support this format and keep the site accessible, we chose a Wikipedia style layout with a side navigation menu. Our goal was to use a straightforward, accessible design for the beginner's guide that wouldn't distract from the information. Once the initial plan was set, we used the usability test to see what our audience thought of these design choices. Because the feedback was positive, we only needed to make minor adjustments. This is how we gathered evidence and insights to understand our audience and guide our design decisions.

## Design Decisions AMY
Based on our user needs, we decided to create a website to serve as a guide. The guide was centered on meeting the needs of beginner non-profit organizations, or users navigating an often intimidating, rigorous funding process. The website prioritizes simplicity and clarity, adopting a clean visual hierarchy that guides users step-by-step through the application process based on the Common Proposal Form. For example, we include a dedicated sidebar region, we separate context via modals "About this guide", and we ensure clear structural signposting. By including example-based breakdowns, the layout ensures that first-time grant writers can easily digest requirements and understand each phase of the Common Proposal Form without getting misled or confused.  
## Testing Process JAMES
We evaluated the guide efficacy through a remote usability test consisting of a digital Google Survey and recruited five participants with varying amounts of experience in grant writing. Users were asked to perform three tasks: using the guide to write out how they would start writing a grant, identifying the major components of a grant, and explaining a grant component of their choice. In addition, users were asked to share their experiences using the guide and evaluate the guide's navigation, readability, and visual appeal. We received valuable feedback concerning the site's usability that guided future revisions. 
## Revisions AMY
Our main revisions were to eliminate confusion that was mentioned in our usability test. Below are our **main revisions** that were made: 
* We linked the referenced document rather than using the encoded version.
* We updated the structure of our guidance by fixing redirection issues within the sidebar region.
* We changed headings to descriptive headings.
* We updated the budget section of our website.
* We tweaked our website layout so that users can find key information faster, and help users scan information quicker.

 Below are our **final revisions** to make: 
* Embed the common proposal form or include images in a separate tab or the same tab under a heading that mentions "problems with accessing the link?"
* Ensure that on mobile devices the navigation bar presents the same components as the navigation bar on the website.
* Final checks on working links, PDF files, link redirections, and mobile-friendly attributes.
