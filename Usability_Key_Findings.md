We received 5 participants, excluding a fake participant meant to test the survey. We had three people who were new to grant writing, and two with moderate to extensive. Averaging all participants, Navigation was ranked 2.4 out of 5, information readability was ranked 2.2 out of 5. Visual appeal ranked 3.2 out of 5, and confidence was ranked 3.0 out of 5.

**Key findings**

- **The Common Proposal Form and Documentation Guidance sections overlap and confuse people.** Both have the same three subtabs (Organizational, Budget, Proposal Info), and users must click into both to get the full picture. Sean suggested merging them or trimming the form to a list without descriptions. Umber asked for an explanation of why both exist.

- **Users want examples.** Umber, Taylor, and Steven all asked for sample content. Steven said "In The proposal information, there is the description of need. The description of need includes did you want to play the approach? What is needed? ". Two specifically asked for an example budget, and Taylor wanted screenshots of real forms, since the only accessible sample form has unique formatting.

- **Beginners need orientation.** Taylor asked for a landing page covering what grants are, the benefits, post-award requirements, and where to find opportunities. Megan said she felt lost and wasn’t sure where answers were in the guide.

- **Navigation is tab-heavy.** Umber found it easy to forget where information lived, and Megan struggled to locate answers. Umber said "There are a lot of tabs to click into which can make it hard to locate specific information and easy to forget which page a specific nugget of information was on."

- **Visual design is a strength.** Four of five praised the look as clean, streamlined, and skimmable, with the green highlighting in the sidebar called out as helpful. Umber Batool said  "It's clear and straightforward, which is good for delivering a lot of info" for the visual design.
